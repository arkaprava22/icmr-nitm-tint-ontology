# Governance and Safety

## 1. Editorial principles

1. Preserve the originating tradition and its terminology.
2. Distinguish documentation of use from evidence of effectiveness.
3. Publish traceable claims, not anonymous advice.
4. Make uncertainty and disagreement visible.
5. Put user safety ahead of completeness or search popularity.
6. Respect community rights, biodiversity, and source licences.

These principles align the design with the WHO Global Traditional Medicine
Strategy 2025-2034 emphasis on evidence, safety, regulation, cultural respect,
sustainability, and qualified practitioners.

## 2. Identity and roles

Practitioner registration and practitioner verification are separate states.
Verification records system of practice, credential issuer, jurisdiction, scope,
expiry, and the staff member who checked it. Public profiles expose only fields
approved for disclosure.

No contributor may approve their own submission. Review assignment should detect
same-organization and declared financial conflicts.

## 3. Review lanes

| Lane | Typical content | Minimum review |
|---|---|---|
| Terminology | Label, synonym, transliteration | Language/terminology reviewer |
| General knowledge | History, ingredient identity | Domain reviewer |
| Health-use claim | Intended use and evidence | Independent domain reviewer |
| Higher risk | Dosage, vulnerable groups, toxicity, interactions | Domain + senior safety reviewer |
| Urgent safety | Suspected serious harm or wrong identity | Immediate hide, then senior review |

Automated checks may identify missing fields, duplicates, inconsistent units,
taxonomic ambiguity, and risky wording. They never approve clinical content.

## 4. Public safety presentation

Every use-claim page must place safety information near the claim, not behind a
secondary tab. The interface must clearly direct users to urgent medical care for
emergency warning signs and must not imply that search results are diagnosis.

High-risk flags include pregnancy, breastfeeding, children, older adults,
allergy, chronic disease, surgery, possible medicine interaction, toxicity,
uncertain biological identity, and prolonged use.

Preparation and dosage are shown only when the editorial board permits the exact
content and audience. A disclaimer does not compensate for an unsafe claim.

## 5. Evidence and wording policy

Public wording is tied to evidence type and reviewer assessment. "Traditionally
used for" must not be rewritten as "proven to treat." Search ranking must not
silently convert popularity, contributor count, or cultural longevity into an
efficacy score.

AI may assist with translation, extraction, duplicate suggestions, and reviewer
summaries. All such output is marked as assisted, linked to its inputs, and
reviewed by a person before publication.

## 6. Traditional knowledge and consent

Before accepting community-held knowledge, capture community/source attribution,
consent basis, permitted audience, permitted reuse, geographic sensitivity, and
benefit-sharing restrictions. Sensitive or sacred knowledge may be metadata-only,
restricted to reviewers, embargoed, or rejected from collection.

## 7. Incident response

An authorized safety reviewer can immediately hide a claim while preserving its
audit record. The incident record captures trigger, affected versions, decision,
notifications, corrective action, and republishing conditions.

Published claims receive risk-based review intervals. New safety evidence can
trigger review before the scheduled date.

## 8. Privacy and security baseline

- Collect only identity and credential data required for trust and operations.
- Separate public practitioner profiles from private verification records.
- Require strong authentication for reviewers and administrators.
- Log privileged actions and export access without logging sensitive content in
  ordinary application logs.
- Define retention and deletion rules before collecting identity documents.
- Run a privacy and security review before pilot practitioner onboarding.

## 9. Governance bodies

- Product and public-interest steering group
- Clinical editorial board representing included systems
- Terminology and ontology working group
- Safety and pharmacovigilance lead
- Language and accessibility reviewers
- Traditional-knowledge/community advisory representation
- Privacy, legal, and security owners

## 10. Phase 1 approval gate

Phase 1 is complete when accountable stakeholders approve the product boundary,
initial systems and languages, practitioner eligibility, public dosage policy,
evidence categories, high-risk review rules, and knowledge-rights policy.
